DATABASE_MEMORIES:
ENSURE: when calculating monthly customer balances from the transactions table, treat both 'withdrawal' and 'purchase' as negative flows by adding txn_type IN ('withdrawal','purchase') to your CASE expression and to any JOIN or WHERE filters  
| CONTEXT: transactions(customer_id, txn_type, amount, transaction_date) must reflect all outflows, not just withdrawals  
| WHEN_TO_CHECK: when aggregating transactions by date_trunc('month', transaction_date) and customer_id  
| EXAMPLE_USAGE:  
Incorrect:  
SELECT customer_id, date_trunc('month', transaction_date) AS mon,  
  SUM(CASE WHEN txn_type='deposit' THEN amount WHEN txn_type='withdrawal' THEN -amount END) AS net  
FROM transactions  
WHERE txn_type IN ('deposit','withdrawal')  
GROUP BY customer_id, mon;  
Correct:  
SELECT customer_id, date_trunc('month', transaction_date) AS mon,  
  SUM(CASE WHEN txn_type='deposit' THEN amount WHEN txn_type IN ('withdrawal','purchase') THEN -amount END) AS net  
FROM transactions  
WHERE txn_type IN ('deposit','withdrawal','purchase')  
GROUP BY customer_id, mon;

ENSURE: to include months with no transactions in your customer month‐end balances, generate a full set of customer×month combinations via CROSS JOIN and then LEFT JOIN transactions with COALESCE on the sum  
| CONTEXT: customers(customer_id) must pair with every month in the reporting range; transactions may be missing for some months  
| WHEN_TO_CHECK: when querying month_end_balances or any per‐customer monthly summary  
| EXAMPLE_USAGE:  
Incorrect:  
SELECT customer_id, date_trunc('month', transaction_date) AS mon, SUM(amount)  
FROM transactions  
GROUP BY customer_id, mon;  
Correct:  
WITH months AS (SELECT generate_series('2022-01-01'::date, '2022-12-01'::date, '1 month') AS mon),  
customer_months AS (SELECT c.customer_id, m.mon FROM customers c CROSS JOIN months m)  
SELECT cm.customer_id, cm.mon,  
  COALESCE(SUM(t.amount),0) AS net_amount  
FROM customer_months cm  
LEFT JOIN transactions t  
  ON t.customer_id=cm.customer_id  
  AND date_trunc('month', t.transaction_date)=cm.mon  
GROUP BY cm.customer_id, cm.mon;

GENERIC_MEMORIES:
VERIFY: when you classify rows into positive and negative values in an aggregation, ensure your CASE expression and any row filters reference the same set of categories  
| PRINCIPLE: mismatched filters and CASE conditions lead to omitted or double-counted data  
| WHEN_TO_APPLY: any time you assign signs or categories to rows before summing  
| EXAMPLE_USAGE:  
If you filter WHERE type IN ('in','out') but only handle 'out' in CASE, you’ll ignore other outflows; instead filter and CASE on the exact same type list.

ENSURE: if you need zero results for missing combinations of keys, build the full key set via CROSS JOIN of dimensions and LEFT JOIN your fact table, then COALESCE aggregated measures to zero  
| PRINCIPLE: direct aggregation skips missing key combinations, leading to gaps  
| WHEN_TO_APPLY: anytime you summarize across all values of one dimension against all values of another (e.g., customers × periods)  
| EXAMPLE_USAGE:  
To report sales per product per month when some combinations have no sales, generate product×month and LEFT JOIN sales before SUM and COALESCE.

VERIFY: after a LEFT JOIN to produce a superset of rows, always wrap aggregate expressions in COALESCE to replace NULLs with an appropriate default  
| PRINCIPLE: SUM over no matching rows returns NULL, not zero  
| WHEN_TO_APPLY: after left-joining a dimension set with a fact table and grouping  
| EXAMPLE_USAGE:  
Wrong: SELECT d.key, SUM(f.value) FROM dims d LEFT JOIN facts f ON d.key=f.key GROUP BY d.key;  
Right: SELECT d.key, COALESCE(SUM(f.value),0) FROM dims d LEFT JOIN facts f ON d.key=f.key GROUP BY d.key.
